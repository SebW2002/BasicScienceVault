# CS13 Reproducibility and Research Software – Worksheet

> Theory: [[CS13 Reproducibility and Research Software]] · Solutions: [[CS13 Reproducibility and Research Software - Solutions]] · Log mistakes in [[Mistakes - Computer Science]]
> No calculator unless marked 🧮. $0.95^{10}\approx0.60$.

## Level 1 – Concepts
1. Define reproducibility, replicability and robustness in one sentence each.
2. What does a git commit contain? Why is the hash useful?
3. Which of these belong in a git repository: analysis scripts, 200 GB of raw Neuropixels data, a `README.md`, a password file, `environment.yml`, figures generated by code, participant names?
4. What does each letter of FAIR stand for?

## Level 2 – Environments and workflows
5. Your analysis gives different results on your laptop and on the cluster. List four possible causes.
6. What is the difference between a virtual environment and a container? When would you use a container?
7. Why can a Jupyter notebook give results that can't be reproduced, even with the same code and data? How do you prevent this?
8. Write a good and a bad commit message for a change that fixes the high-pass cutoff of a filter.

## Level 3 – Open science
9. A researcher tries 10 different reasonable analysis pipelines and reports the one with p < 0.05. If the null is true and the analyses were independent, what is the probability of finding at least one "significant" result?
10. What is the difference between preregistration and a registered report?
11. Is it allowed to run exploratory analyses after preregistering? How should they be reported?
12. Why can data be FAIR but not open?

## Level 4 – Ethics and data protection
13. Classify as anonymised or pseudonymised: a) MRI data where names are replaced by codes and the key is stored in a locked cabinet at the hospital, b) aggregated group means over 200 subjects, c) a defaced MRI with exact birth date, rare diagnosis and town of residence.
14. You want to share structural MRI scans from a study on OpenNeuro. Name three steps you must take.
15. Write a BibTeX entry for a fictional article: authors Ada Lovelace and Alan Turing, title "Neural Codes", journal "Journal of Imaginary Neuroscience", year 2026, volume 3, pages 1–10, DOI 10.0000/jin.2026.001.
16. 🧮 With 20 independent analysis paths, compute $1 - 0.95^{20}$.
