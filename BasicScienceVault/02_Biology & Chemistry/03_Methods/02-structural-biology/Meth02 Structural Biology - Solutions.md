# Meth02 Structural Biology – Solutions

> Worksheet: [[Meth02 Structural Biology - Worksheet]] · Theory: [[Meth02 Structural Biology]] · Wrong? → [[Mistakes - Methods]]

## Level 1
1. 3 Å = 0.3 nm; 0.2 nm = 2 Å. Side chains need about 3 Å or better (clearly at < 2.5 Å).
2. Detectors record only the **intensities** (squared amplitudes) of the diffracted waves, not their **phases**. Both are needed to compute the electron density by Fourier transform. Phases come from extra experiments (heavy atoms, anomalous scattering) or from a known similar structure (molecular replacement).
3. Proteins in solution (no crystal, near physiological conditions); information on **dynamics** and flexibility; works for disordered proteins; direct study of binding and folding.
4. ESI = electrospray ionisation: a gentle method that produces many charge states, couples to LC and works from liquids. MALDI = matrix-assisted laser desorption/ionisation: a laser and matrix crystals produce mostly singly charged ions, from solid spots or tissue sections (imaging MS).
5. Peptides are selected and **fragmented**. The fragment masses reveal the **sequence** (and the position of modifications), allowing confident identification.

## Level 2
6. $d = \frac{\lambda}{2\sin\theta} = \frac{1.5}{2\cdot0.5} = 1.5\,\text{Å}$
7. $\sin90° = 1$, so $d = \lambda/2 = 0.75\,\text{Å}$. Since $\sin\theta\le1$, smaller spacings can't produce a reflection; $\lambda/2$ is the limit. (In practice, resolution is limited much earlier by crystal order.)
8. $42.6\cdot14.1\approx600\,\text{MHz}$ (a "600 MHz spectrometer"). At 3 T: about 128 MHz.
9. $\frac{20\,000 + 20}{20} = 1001$
10. +80 Da (HPO₃). MS/MS fragments locate **which** residue carries the phosphate (site localisation).

## Level 3
11. a) **Cryo-EM SPA** (large, heteromeric membrane protein). b) **NMR** (small, dynamic, disordered). c) **Phosphoproteomics by LC-MS/MS** (e.g. with TMT labels). d) **X-ray crystallography**. e) **Cryo-EM** of sarkosyl-insoluble filaments extracted from brain.
12. Predictions show one (often the most common) conformation and are less reliable for **state changes** (open, closed, inactivated), **ligand/drug binding**, **lipid interactions**, assemblies with auxiliary subunits and **disease mutations**. Experimental validation is needed for mechanisms and drug design.

## Level 4
13. The structure showed a narrow **selectivity filter** lined by **backbone carbonyl oxygens** (signature sequence TVGYG). These oxygens are arranged like the water molecules around a K⁺ ion, so K⁺ can shed its water at almost no energy cost. Na⁺ is too small to contact all of them at once, so shedding its water would be energetically costly and it does not pass.
14. Different folds mean distinct "strains" that probably spread and damage differently. For **diagnosis**: PET tracers and antibodies can be designed against specific folds (classification of tauopathies by structure). For **therapy**: drugs or antibodies must recognise the relevant fold; models that use recombinant filaments with the wrong fold may give misleading results.
15. 1+: $1234.6 + 1.007 = 1235.6$. 2+: $(1234.6 + 2.014)/2 = 618.3$. 3+: $(1234.6 + 3.021)/3\approx412.5$.
