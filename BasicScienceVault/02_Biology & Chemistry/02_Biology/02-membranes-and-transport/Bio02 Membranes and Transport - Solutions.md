# Bio02 Membranes and Transport – Solutions

> Worksheet: [[Bio02 Membranes and Transport - Worksheet]] · Theory: [[Bio02 Membranes and Transport]] · Wrong? → [[Mistakes - Biology]]

## Level 1
1. O₂, CO₂ and ethanol cross easily. Na⁺, glucose and glutamate don't (they need channels or transporters).
2. 3 Na⁺ out, 2 K⁺ in, 1 ATP hydrolysed.
3. Voltage-gated: Nav1.6, Kv1, Cav2.1. Ligand-gated: AMPA, GABA_A, nAChR. Mechanosensitive: Piezo2, TMC1.
4. Higher temperature, more unsaturated (cis) fatty acids, shorter chains. (Cholesterol has a buffering effect.)

## Level 2
5. Channels: open pore, about 10⁶–10⁸ ions/s, no saturation in the physiological range. Carriers: bind their substrate and change conformation for each transport cycle, about 10²–10⁴/s, saturable (they have a $K_m$).
6. Each cycle moves 3 positive charges out and 2 in, a **net outward current** of one charge, which hyperpolarises the membrane directly by a few mV.
7. EAATs couple glutamate uptake to the co-transport of 3 Na⁺ and 1 H⁺ (with 1 K⁺ out). The large inward electrochemical gradient of Na⁺ provides the energy to move glutamate uphill, up to a gradient of about 10⁴–10⁶. This is secondary active transport.
8. The **S4 segment** of each domain carries positively charged Arg/Lys residues every third position. When the membrane depolarises, the field pushes S4 outward, and this movement is coupled to the pore gate. The movement of these charges can be measured as a "gating current".
9. An "eat me" signal for phagocytes (microglia, macrophages), for example in apoptotic cells or in synapses tagged for pruning.

## Level 3
10. $i = 20\,\text{pS}\cdot(-80 - 0)\,\text{mV} = -1600\times10^{-15}\,\text{A} = -1.6\,\text{pA}$. Negative means **inward** (cations flow in).
11. $I = N\cdot P_o\cdot i = 1000\cdot0.1\cdot(-1.6\,\text{pA}) = -160\,\text{pA}$
12. $C = 10^{-5}\cdot1\,\mu\text{F} = 10^{-11}\,\text{F} = 10\,\text{pF}$. $\tau = 10^8\,\Omega\cdot10^{-11}\,\text{F} = 10^{-3}\,\text{s} = 1\,\text{ms}$.

## Level 4
13. **Cocaine** *blocks* DAT, so reuptake stops and dopamine stays in the cleft longer. **Amphetamine** is a DAT *substrate*: it is taken up, empties vesicles via VMAT2, raises cytosolic dopamine and makes DAT run **in reverse**, releasing dopamine independently of action potentials.
14. Nav1.1 is especially important in **fast-spiking inhibitory interneurons** (e.g. parvalbumin cells). Without it, these cells can't sustain high-frequency firing, so inhibition in the network collapses and excitatory neurons are **disinhibited**, which leads to seizures. Network effects can reverse single-cell intuitions.
15. Initially there is little change, because a single AP barely changes the concentrations. Over minutes, however, Na⁺ accumulates inside and K⁺ outside. $E_\text{K}$ and $E_\text{Na}$ move towards 0, the cell depolarises, Na⁺ channels inactivate and the cell stops firing. Secondary transporters fail (no Na⁺ gradient), Ca²⁺ rises (NCX reverses), the cell swells osmotically and dies.
16. $6\times10^7$ Na⁺, with 3 Na⁺ per cycle, is $2\times10^7$ cycles. At 100 cycles/s per pump that requires $2\times10^5$ pumps for 1 s. Real neurons have millions of pumps, and a large fraction of neuronal ATP is used this way.
