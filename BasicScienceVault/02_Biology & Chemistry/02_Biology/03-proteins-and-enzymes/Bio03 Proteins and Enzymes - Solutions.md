# Bio03 Proteins and Enzymes – Solutions

> Worksheet: [[Bio03 Proteins and Enzymes - Worksheet]] · Theory: [[Bio03 Proteins and Enzymes]] · Wrong? → [[Mistakes - Biology]]

## Level 1
1. Primary: sequence (peptide bonds). Secondary: α-helix and β-sheet (backbone H-bonds). Tertiary: 3D fold (hydrophobic core, side-chain H-bonds, salt bridges, disulfides). Quaternary: assembly of subunits (the same interactions between chains).
2. a) amyloid-β and tau b) α-synuclein c) huntingtin (polyQ) d) PrP (PrP^Sc)
3. D1: Gs. D2: Gi. µ-opioid: Gi/o. mGluR5: Gq. β-adrenergic: Gs.
4. An agonist binds and activates (full response). An antagonist binds without activating and blocks agonists. A partial agonist activates, but even at full occupancy gives a sub-maximal response, and it can partially block full agonists.

## Level 2
5. $20\cdot0.15\,\text{nm} = 3\,\text{nm}$, about the thickness of the hydrophobic core of the membrane. A helix satisfies all backbone H-bonds internally (no free polar C=O or N–H in the lipid), and hydrophobic side chains face the lipid tails.
6. (1) Ligand binds and the receptor changes conformation. (2) The receptor makes Gα exchange GDP for GTP. (3) Gα-GTP and Gβγ separate and regulate effectors (adenylyl cyclase, PLC, ion channels). (4) Gα hydrolyses GTP to GDP (accelerated by RGS proteins) and reassociates with βγ. (5) GRKs phosphorylate the receptor, β-arrestin binds, and the receptor is desensitised and internalised.
7. The misfolded protein PrP^Sc acts as a **template**: it binds normal PrP^C and converts it into the misfolded conformation. The information is stored in the protein's **shape**, not in a sequence. Each new PrP^Sc converts more molecules, an autocatalytic chain reaction.
8. $V_\text{max}$ is the maximal rate when all enzyme is saturated; $K_m$ is the [S] at half-maximal rate (a measure of affinity, small $K_m$ = high affinity). Competitive: $K_m$ ↑, $V_\text{max}$ unchanged. Non-competitive: $V_\text{max}$ ↓, $K_m$ unchanged.

## Level 3
9. $[S] = K_m$: $\frac{K_m}{2K_m} = \frac12$. $3K_m$: $\frac34$. $K_m/4$: $\frac{1/4}{5/4} = \frac15$. $99K_m$: $\frac{99}{100}$.
10. 50 %: $[L] = K_d = 2\,\text{nM}$. 90 %: $\frac{[L]}{[L] + 2} = 0.9 \Rightarrow [L] = 0.9[L] + 1.8 \Rightarrow [L] = 18\,\text{nM}$ (i.e. $9K_d$).
11. $K_m^\text{app} = K_m(1 + 1) = 2K_m$, so half-maximal rate now needs $[S] = 2K_m$.
12. $n = 4$: θ$(K/2) = \frac{1/16}{1/16 + 1} = \frac1{17}\approx0.06$; θ$(K) = 0.5$; θ$(2K) = \frac{16}{17}\approx0.94$.
    $n = 1$: $\frac13\approx0.33$; $0.5$; $\frac23\approx0.67$.
    High cooperativity creates a **switch**: a fourfold change in concentration gives an almost all-or-none response (with $n = 4$, from 6 % to 94 %). This is why transmitter release depends steeply on Ca²⁺, roughly as $[\text{Ca}^{2+}]^4$.

## Level 4
13. AChE normally removes ACh within ms. Partial inhibition keeps ACh in the cleft longer and at higher concentration, so the remaining (degenerating) cholinergic inputs produce a stronger effect.
14. GPCRs act through a **chain of steps** (G-protein activation, enzyme activity, second messengers, kinases, phosphorylation of targets), each taking time. **Amplification** at each step and the persistence of second messengers and phosphorylation keep effects going long after the ligand is gone.
15. Its **ceiling effect**: even at full occupancy, buprenorphine activates the receptor only partially, so respiratory depression plateaus. Combined with high affinity, it **displaces** heroin (a full agonist) from the receptors and lowers the total activation, which triggers withdrawal.
16. Opening GIRK channels increases K⁺ conductance, which hyperpolarises the cell and lowers input resistance (shunting). Lower cAMP/PKA activity reduces the phosphorylation of channels and receptors. Overall, the neurons become **less excitable** (D2 is inhibitory for indirect-pathway medium spiny neurons, see [[Bio07 Neural Circuits and Systems]]).
