# Bio07 Neural Circuits and Systems – Worksheet

> Theory: [[Bio07 Neural Circuits and Systems]] · Solutions: [[Bio07 Neural Circuits and Systems - Solutions]] · Log mistakes in [[Mistakes - Biology]]
> No calculator needed.

## Level 1 – Recall
1. Define: rostral, caudal, dorsal, ventral, medial, lateral, contralateral.
2. Which section shows both hemispheres from the front: sagittal, coronal or horizontal?
3. Name the main function of: hippocampus, cerebellum, basal ganglia, thalamus, hypothalamus, amygdala.
4. Name the four glial cell types of the CNS and one function of each.

## Level 2 – Circuits
5. Draw the hippocampal trisynaptic circuit with the names of the fibre pathways.
6. Which cortical layer receives most thalamic input? Which sends output to subcortical structures?
7. Describe the direct and indirect pathways of the basal ganglia and how dopamine affects each.
8. What makes climbing fibres special in the cerebellum?

## Level 3 – Development
9. Put in order: synaptogenesis, neurulation, migration, myelination, neurogenesis, axon guidance, pruning.
10. Cortical layers form "inside out". Which layer is generated first, and which last?
11. Why does folic acid supplementation reduce neural tube defects?
12. Hubel and Wiesel sutured one eye shut in kittens during a critical period. What happened, and why didn't it happen in adult cats?

## Level 4 – Neuro transfer
13. Explain why the loss of dopamine neurons in Parkinson's disease leads to *reduced* movement although dopamine acts on both pathways.
14. Patient H.M. could still learn new motor skills (mirror drawing) after his hippocampal resection. What does this tell us about memory systems?
15. Microglia are not derived from neural tissue. Where do they come from, and why is this relevant for understanding brain immunity?
16. In the circuit A ⊣ B ⊣ C (A inhibits B, B inhibits C), what is the effect of activating A on C? Name a real example of this motif in the basal ganglia.
