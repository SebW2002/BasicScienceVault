# Bio07 Neural Circuits and Systems – Solutions

> Worksheet: [[Bio07 Neural Circuits and Systems - Worksheet]] · Theory: [[Bio07 Neural Circuits and Systems]] · Wrong? → [[Mistakes - Biology]]

## Level 1
1. Rostral: towards the nose/front. Caudal: towards the tail/back. Dorsal: towards the back/top. Ventral: towards the belly/bottom. Medial: towards the midline. Lateral: away from the midline. Contralateral: on the opposite side.
2. Coronal (frontal).
3. Hippocampus: episodic memory, spatial navigation. Cerebellum: motor coordination and learning, timing. Basal ganglia: action selection, reinforcement learning. Thalamus: sensory relay and gating to cortex. Hypothalamus: homeostasis, hormones. Amygdala: emotion, fear learning.
4. Astrocytes (K⁺ and glutamate uptake, BBB, synapse support), oligodendrocytes (CNS myelin), microglia (immune defence, pruning), ependymal cells (line ventricles, CSF). (OPCs/NG2 cells are also often listed.)

## Level 2
5. Entorhinal cortex –(perforant path)→ dentate gyrus –(mossy fibres)→ CA3 –(Schaffer collaterals)→ CA1 → subiculum → back to the entorhinal cortex. (CA3 also has strong recurrent collaterals, an auto-associative network.)
6. Layer 4 receives thalamic input. Layer 5 projects subcortically (and layer 6 projects to the thalamus).
7. Direct: cortex → striatum (D1 MSNs) ⊣ GPi/SNr ⊣ thalamus → cortex. The net effect disinhibits the thalamus, facilitating movement ("go"). Indirect: striatum (D2 MSNs) ⊣ GPe ⊣ STN → GPi/SNr ⊣ thalamus. The net effect increases inhibition of the thalamus ("no-go"). Dopamine **excites D1** (strengthens "go") and **inhibits D2** (weakens "no-go"); both effects promote movement.
8. Each Purkinje cell receives input from just **one** climbing fibre (from the inferior olive), but that fibre forms hundreds of synapses on it and triggers a **complex spike**. Climbing fibres signal errors and induce LTD at parallel fibre synapses, the basis of cerebellar motor learning.

## Level 3
9. Neurulation → neurogenesis → migration → axon guidance → synaptogenesis → pruning → myelination (they overlap in time).
10. First: **layer 6** (deepest, after the preplate/layer 1 pioneer cells). Last: **layer 2** (outermost of the cellular layers). Later-born neurons migrate past earlier ones.
11. Folate is needed for nucleotide synthesis and methylation. Rapid cell division during neural tube closure needs a lot of DNA synthesis, so folate deficiency impairs closure.
12. Cortical neurons in V1 stopped responding to the deprived eye; ocular dominance shifted towards the open eye. The effect persisted. In adults, the critical period has closed (mature inhibition, perineuronal nets, myelin-associated brakes), so the same deprivation has only small effects.

## Level 4
13. Loss of dopamine **weakens the direct pathway** (less D1 excitation) and **strengthens the indirect pathway** (less D2 inhibition). Both changes increase GPi/SNr inhibition of the thalamus, so cortical motor programmes are suppressed, causing bradykinesia and akinesia.
14. Memory is not a single system. **Declarative/episodic** memory depends on the medial temporal lobe; **procedural** memory depends on other structures (basal ganglia, cerebellum, motor cortex).
15. From **yolk-sac macrophage precursors**, which colonise the brain early in embryonic development. They are a distinct, self-renewing immune population, not neural cells and not replaced by blood monocytes under normal conditions. Their genes and responses differ from peripheral macrophages, which matters for inflammation, pruning and diseases (*TREM2* in Alzheimer's).
16. Activating A inhibits B, which **disinhibits** C, so C becomes **more active** (two negatives make a positive). Example: striatum (D1 MSN) ⊣ GPi/SNr ⊣ thalamus, so activating the striatum releases thalamic activity (the direct pathway).
