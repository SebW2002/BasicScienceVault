# Bio05 Neurons and Action Potentials – Solutions

> Worksheet: [[Bio05 Neurons and Action Potentials - Worksheet]] · Theory: [[Bio05 Neurons and Action Potentials]] · Wrong? → [[Mistakes - Biology]]

## Level 1
1. Dendrites (spines) → soma → axon hillock / AIS → axon (myelin, nodes) → axon terminals.
2. CNS: oligodendrocytes, one cell myelinating segments of **many** axons. PNS: Schwann cells, one cell per **single** internode. (Schwann cells also support regeneration better.)
3. Depolarisation to threshold (synaptic/injected current, first Na⁺) → rising phase (Na⁺ in) → repolarisation (K⁺ out, Nav inactivated) → afterhyperpolarisation (K⁺ out, below rest) → return to rest.
4. Absolute: no AP possible, because Nav channels are inactivated. Relative: an AP is possible but needs a stronger stimulus, because some Nav channels are still inactivated and K⁺ conductance is high.

## Level 2
5. At rest, the membrane is far more permeable to K⁺ than to Na⁺ (about 25:1 or more). In the GHK equation, the ion with the highest permeability dominates.
6. Depolarisation opens Nav, Na⁺ influx depolarises further, and more Nav open (a self-reinforcing loop). It ends because (1) Nav **inactivate** within about 1 ms, (2) delayed Kv channels open, and (3) $V_m$ approaches $E_\text{Na}$, so the driving force for Na⁺ shrinks.
7. The AIS has a very high density of Nav channels (especially Nav1.6, with a lower activation threshold) and electrically small compartments, so it reaches threshold first. Dendrites have fewer Nav channels and a large surface area.
8. The membrane behind the AP is refractory (Nav inactivated), so the local current can only excite the membrane ahead. (If an axon is stimulated in the middle, APs run in both directions: antidromic conduction.)
9. a) TTX blocks Nav, so there is **no AP** (only passive responses). b) TEA blocks Kv, so repolarisation is slowed and the **AP is much broader** (and the AHP is reduced).

## Level 3
10. $10\cdot6 = 60\,\text{m/s}$, so 1 m takes $\frac{1}{60}\,\text{s}\approx17\,\text{ms}$.
11. 1 s, about 60 times slower. This explains "first pain, second pain": a sharp first pain via Aδ fibres and a dull, delayed second pain via C fibres.
12. After 1 mm: $e^{-1}\approx37\%$. After 2 mm: $e^{-2}\approx14\%$.
13. $\lambda\propto\sqrt{r_m}$, so $\lambda$ doubles ($\sqrt4 = 2$).
14. $v\propto\sqrt d$, so twice the speed needs $d$ four times larger. Hence myelin: in myelinated axons $v\propto d$, so twice the diameter gives twice the speed, and much thinner fibres are fast.

## Level 4
15. Demyelination lowers membrane resistance and raises capacitance at the affected internodes, so current leaks and more charge is needed. The next node reaches threshold more slowly (delay) or not at all (conduction block). Some axons switch to slow continuous conduction by redistributing Nav channels. The overall latency of the visual signal (P100 wave) increases.
16. **Kv3 channels**: activate quickly at relatively depolarised voltages and close quickly, so APs are very narrow and the AHP is short. Fast Nav recovery from inactivation (Nav1.1). High density of channels. Together, short APs plus short refractory periods allow high rates.
17. $t = -20\ln(1 - 15/20) = -20\ln(0.25) = 20\ln4\approx20\cdot1.386\approx27.7\,\text{ms}$
