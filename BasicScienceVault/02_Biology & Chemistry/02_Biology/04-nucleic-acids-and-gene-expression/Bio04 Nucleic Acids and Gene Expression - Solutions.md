# Bio04 Nucleic Acids and Gene Expression – Solutions

> Worksheet: [[Bio04 Nucleic Acids and Gene Expression - Worksheet]] · Theory: [[Bio04 Nucleic Acids and Gene Expression]] · Wrong? → [[Mistakes - Biology]]

## Level 1
1. DNA → DNA (DNA polymerase), DNA → RNA (RNA polymerase, Pol II for mRNA), RNA → protein (ribosome). Also reverse transcription (reverse transcriptase).
2. 5' capping, splicing (removing introns), 3' polyadenylation.
3. Each daughter double helix has one original (parental) and one newly synthesised strand.
4. Activating: histone acetylation, H3K4 trimethylation. Repressing: DNA (CpG) methylation, H3K27/H3K9 methylation, HDAC activity.

## Level 2
5. mRNA (complementary to the template, U for T): 5'-AUG UUU GGC AAA UAG-3' → **Met–Phe–Gly–Lys–stop**.
   (Check: template 3'-TAC → mRNA 5'-AUG ✓; AAA → UUU; CCG → GGC; TTT → AAA; ATC → UAG.)
6. UUU → UUC: **silent** (still Phe). UGG (Trp) → UGA (stop): **nonsense**, which truncates the protein.
7. A **frameshift**: all downstream codons are read wrong, producing a nonsense amino-acid sequence and usually an early stop codon. The protein is almost always non-functional.
8. DNA polymerase can only **extend** an existing 3'-OH end, not start a strand from scratch, so primase lays down an RNA primer. Because polymerase only synthesises 5' → 3' and the strands are antiparallel, one strand must be built "backwards" in short fragments (Okazaki fragments) as the replication fork opens.

## Level 3
9. $4\cdot3\cdot2 = 24$ isoforms
10. Activity lets Ca²⁺ enter (NMDA receptors, L-type Ca²⁺ channels). Ca²⁺/calmodulin activates kinases (CaMKIV, PKA via cAMP, MAPK/ERK), which phosphorylate CREB in the nucleus. CREB recruits CBP (a histone acetyltransferase), and immediate early genes are transcribed (c-Fos, Arc, ...). Their products regulate late genes that stabilise synapses. c-Fos is expressed rapidly and transiently (mRNA peaks at ~30 min, protein at ~1–2 h) and specifically after strong activity, so staining for it shows which neurons were active.
11. HDAC inhibitors keep histones **acetylated**, which keeps chromatin open at memory-related genes (e.g. those activated by CREB/CBP). These genes are transcribed more strongly or for longer, which supports the protein synthesis needed for consolidation.

## Level 4
12. CAG codes for **glutamine** (Q), giving a polyglutamine tract. The repeat unit has 3 bases, so adding whole repeats keeps the reading frame intact. Downstream, the protein is normal; it just contains a long polyQ stretch that promotes misfolding.
13. Patients have lost *SMN1* but have *SMN2*, which is nearly identical but whose pre-mRNA mostly **skips exon 7**. Nusinersen binds an intronic splicing silencer and promotes exon 7 inclusion, so *SMN2* now produces full-length, functional SMN. It works because a **backup gene exists**; with a complete deletion and no backup, splicing modulation would have nothing to act on (gene replacement, e.g. AAV9-*SMN1*, would be needed).
14. DNA is damaged constantly by ROS (high metabolic rate), by spontaneous base loss and deamination, and even by activity-induced breaks. Neurons must keep their genome intact for **decades** and can't be replaced by dividing cells. Defects in DNA repair lead to the slow accumulation of damage, transcription errors and cell death, which explains why many repair-deficiency syndromes are neurodegenerative.
15. Any two: different **transcription factor** combinations, **epigenetic** states (methylation, histone marks), **alternative splicing** of channels and receptors, **mRNA localisation and local translation**, miRNA regulation, and post-translational modification. The outcome is a different set of ion channels with different properties.
