# Bio01 Cell Biology – Solutions

> Worksheet: [[Bio01 Cell Biology - Worksheet]] · Theory: [[Bio01 Cell Biology]] · Wrong? → [[Mistakes - Biology]]

## Level 1
1. Any three: nucleus (no/yes), membrane-bound organelles (no/yes), size (~1–10 vs. ~10–100 µm), ribosomes (70S vs. 80S), DNA (circular vs. linear chromosomes with histones), cytoskeleton (simple vs. complex).
2. a) mitochondria b) smooth ER c) Golgi apparatus d) lysosomes
3. Actin filaments ~7 nm; intermediate filaments (neurofilaments) ~10 nm; microtubules ~25 nm.
4. Kinesin (anterograde, towards the plus end). Dynein (retrograde, towards the minus end).

## Level 2
5. Gene (nucleus) → transcription → mRNA export → translation at the **rough ER** (co-translational insertion into the membrane, folding, N-glycosylation, assembly of subunits) → vesicles to the **Golgi** (glycan processing, sorting) → vesicle transport along microtubules into dendrites → exocytosis to the plasma membrane → lateral diffusion into the synapse, where it is anchored by PSD-95 and auxiliary proteins.
6. Own circular DNA, 70S-like ribosomes, a double membrane (the inner one bacteria-like, e.g. with cardiolipin), division by fission, and sequence similarity to α-proteobacteria.
7. Vesicles can no longer pinch off (endocytosis is blocked), so the vesicle membrane stays in the plasma membrane and is not recycled. During high-frequency firing, the vesicle pool **depletes** and transmission fails. (Drosophila *shibire* mutants, which carry a temperature-sensitive dynamin, become paralysed at high temperature.)
8. Nissl substance is the rough ER (ribosomal RNA, which binds basic dyes such as cresyl violet). It is abundant in the soma and proximal dendrites but largely absent from the axon (axon hillock and axon).

## Level 3
9. Fast: $1000\,\text{mm} / 400\,\text{mm/day} = 2.5$ days. Slow: 1000 days ≈ 2.7 years.
10. Neurofilaments are abundant structural proteins found almost exclusively in neurons, especially large myelinated axons. When axons are damaged, NfL is released into the CSF and some of it reaches the blood. It is therefore specific for neurons and proportional to the amount of damage, though not specific to any one disease.
11. **Botulinum** acts at the neuromuscular junction: it blocks ACh release onto muscles, so they can't contract (flaccid paralysis). **Tetanus** toxin is taken up at the same terminals but transported **retrogradely** into the spinal cord, where it blocks release from **inhibitory** interneurons (glycine/GABA). Motor neurons are then disinhibited, causing continuous contraction (spastic paralysis, lockjaw).

## Level 4
12. Microtubules become unstable, so the transport tracks fall apart. Transport of mitochondria, vesicles and proteins to the synapse fails, causing energy deficits and synapse loss. Aggregated tau (neurofibrillary tangles) is toxic in its own right and can spread between neurons. In the end, axons degenerate and neurons die.
13. Dividing cells "dilute" damaged proteins and organelles with every division. Neurons can't, so they accumulate damage over decades unless they actively break it down. Autophagy and lysosomes are their main cleaning system, and defects lead to protein aggregates and neurodegeneration.
14. **Speed** (no transport over long distances), **specificity** (proteins are made only where the synapse is active, which supports input specificity of plasticity), and **efficiency** (one mRNA produces many protein copies on site).
