# Bio01 Cell Biology – Worksheet

> Theory: [[Bio01 Cell Biology]] · Solutions: [[Bio01 Cell Biology - Solutions]] · Log mistakes in [[Mistakes - Biology]]
> No calculator needed.

## Level 1 – Recall
1. Name three differences between prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells.
2. Which organelle a) makes ATP, b) stores Ca²⁺, c) sorts and glycosylates proteins, d) degrades material at pH ≈ 5?
3. Name the three cytoskeletal filament types with their approximate diameters.
4. Which motor protein transports cargo from the soma to the synapse? Which transports in the opposite direction?

## Level 2 – Understanding
5. Describe the path of a newly synthesised AMPA receptor from its gene to the postsynaptic membrane (organelles in order).
6. What evidence supports the idea that mitochondria were once bacteria?
7. What happens if dynamin is blocked at a synapse that is firing at high frequency?
8. Why do Nissl stains label neuronal cell bodies but not axons?

## Level 3 – Apply
9. Fast axonal transport moves at about 400 mm/day. How long does it take a mitochondrion to travel from a motor neuron soma in the spinal cord to the foot (1 m)? With slow transport at 1 mm/day?
10. Neurofilament light chain (NfL) concentrations in blood rise after traumatic brain injury and in MS. Why is NfL a good marker for axon damage?
11. Botulinum toxin and tetanus toxin both cleave SNARE proteins, but they cause opposite symptoms (flaccid vs. spastic paralysis). Explain.

## Level 4 – Neuro transfer
12. Tau stabilises microtubules in axons. Predict the consequences for a neuron if tau detaches and aggregates (as in Alzheimer's disease).
13. Why are neurons, as long-lived non-dividing cells, especially dependent on autophagy and lysosomal degradation?
14. Long-term potentiation at a single synapse can depend on proteins made locally in the dendrite. What advantage does local translation have compared with making all proteins in the soma?
