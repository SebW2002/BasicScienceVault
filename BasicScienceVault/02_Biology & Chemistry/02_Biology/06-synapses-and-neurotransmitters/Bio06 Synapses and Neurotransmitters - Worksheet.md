# Bio06 Synapses and Neurotransmitters – Worksheet

> Theory: [[Bio06 Synapses and Neurotransmitters]] · Solutions: [[Bio06 Synapses and Neurotransmitters - Solutions]] · Log mistakes in [[Mistakes - Biology]]
> No calculator needed.

## Level 1 – Recall
1. List four differences between chemical and electrical synapses.
2. Name the precursor and rate-limiting enzyme for dopamine and serotonin, and the synthesis enzyme for GABA and ACh.
3. Which receptor types are ionotropic: nAChR, mGluR5, GABA_A, D2, 5-HT₃, AMPA, muscarinic M1?
4. How is the signal terminated for ACh, and for glutamate?

## Level 2 – Understanding
5. List the steps of chemical transmission from presynaptic AP to postsynaptic potential.
6. What three conditions must be met for an NMDA receptor to conduct? Why is it called a coincidence detector?
7. Explain the opposite outcomes of high-frequency and low-frequency stimulation in terms of Ca²⁺ and enzymes.
8. What is meant by input specificity, cooperativity and associativity of LTP?
9. Why can a GABA_A synapse inhibit a neuron even if it doesn't hyperpolarise it?

## Level 3 – Quantitative
10. A synapse has $n = 10$ release sites with $p = 0.3$ each. Mean number of vesicles released per AP? Probability that **no** vesicle is released? (Use $0.7^{10}\approx0.028$.)
11. Release scales roughly as $[\text{Ca}^{2+}]^4$. By what factor does release change if Ca²⁺ influx is a) halved, b) increased by 50 %?
12. STDP: $\Delta w = 0.01\,e^{-\Delta t/20\,\text{ms}}$ for pre-before-post. Compare $\Delta w$ for $\Delta t = 0$, 20 ms and 60 ms (use $e^{-1}\approx0.37$, $e^{-3}\approx0.05$).
13. The synaptic delay is about 0.5 ms. A reflex arc has 3 synapses and 1.5 m of axon at 60 m/s. Estimate the total conduction time. Which part dominates?

## Level 4 – Neuro transfer
14. Classify each drug by its point of action (synthesis, storage, release, reuptake, degradation, receptor): fluoxetine, cocaine, L-DOPA, reserpine, donepezil, diazepam, haloperidol, amphetamine.
15. Why does too much glutamate kill neurons? Why is memantine (a weak, voltage-dependent NMDA blocker) better tolerated than a complete NMDA block?
16. In myasthenia gravis, patients make antibodies against nicotinic receptors at the neuromuscular junction. Why do muscles tire quickly with repeated use? Why do AChE inhibitors help?
17. An experiment blocks protein synthesis immediately after a learning trial. Early LTP is normal but late LTP and long-term memory fail. Interpret.
