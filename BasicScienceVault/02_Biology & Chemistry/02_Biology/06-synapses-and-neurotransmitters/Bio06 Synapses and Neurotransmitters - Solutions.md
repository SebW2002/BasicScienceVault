# Bio06 Synapses and Neurotransmitters – Solutions

> Worksheet: [[Bio06 Synapses and Neurotransmitters - Worksheet]] · Theory: [[Bio06 Synapses and Neurotransmitters]] · Wrong? → [[Mistakes - Biology]]

## Level 1
1. Any four: cleft width (20–40 nm vs. 3.5 nm), mechanism (transmitter vs. direct ion flow), speed (0.5–1 ms delay vs. none), direction (one-way vs. bidirectional), sign/amplification (both signs and gain vs. same sign and attenuation), plasticity (rich vs. limited).
2. Dopamine: tyrosine, tyrosine hydroxylase. Serotonin: tryptophan, tryptophan hydroxylase. GABA: glutamate decarboxylase (GAD). ACh: choline acetyltransferase (ChAT).
3. Ionotropic: nAChR, GABA_A, 5-HT₃, AMPA. (mGluR5, D2 and M1 are GPCRs.)
4. ACh: hydrolysed by AChE in the cleft. Glutamate: reuptake via EAATs, mostly into astrocytes (where it becomes glutamine).

## Level 2
5. AP depolarises the terminal → Cav channels open → Ca²⁺ influx → Ca²⁺ binds synaptotagmin → SNARE-mediated vesicle fusion → transmitter diffuses across the cleft → binds postsynaptic receptors → ion flow (or second-messenger cascade) → EPSP/IPSP → termination and vesicle recycling.
6. (1) Glutamate is bound, (2) glycine or D-serine is bound, (3) the membrane is depolarised enough to expel Mg²⁺ from the pore. It only conducts when the presynaptic cell releases (glutamate) **and** the postsynaptic cell is active (depolarised) at the same time, so it detects coincidence.
7. High frequency causes strong NMDA receptor activation and **high** Ca²⁺, which activates kinases (CaMKII, PKC). AMPA receptors are phosphorylated and inserted, giving LTP. Low frequency causes a **moderate**, prolonged Ca²⁺ rise, which preferentially activates high-affinity **phosphatases** (calcineurin, PP1). AMPA receptors are dephosphorylated and removed, giving LTD.
8. Input specificity: only active synapses are potentiated, not the neighbours. Cooperativity: a threshold amount of coactive input is needed. Associativity: a weak input becomes potentiated when paired with a strong input on the same cell.
9. **Shunting inhibition**: open Cl⁻ channels with $E_\text{Cl}\approx V_\text{rest}$ raise membrane conductance. Excitatory currents then leak out through these channels, so EPSPs are smaller and the cell has difficulty reaching threshold.

## Level 3
10. Mean $= np = 3$ vesicles. $P(0) = (1-p)^n = 0.7^{10}\approx0.028$, about 3 %.
11. a) $(1/2)^4 = 1/16$: release drops to about 6 %. b) $1.5^4 = 5.06$: about a fivefold increase. Small changes in Ca²⁺ influx (e.g. by presynaptic GPCRs or channel modulation) have huge effects.
12. $\Delta t = 0$: 0.01. 20 ms: $0.01\cdot e^{-1}\approx0.0037$. 60 ms: $0.01\cdot e^{-3}\approx0.0005$. Plasticity is effectively restricted to a window of a few tens of ms.
13. Axon: $1.5/60 = 25\,\text{ms}$. Synapses: $3\cdot0.5 = 1.5\,\text{ms}$. Total ≈ 26.5 ms; **axonal conduction dominates**. (Real reflex times are longer because of receptor and muscle delays.)

## Level 4
14. Fluoxetine: reuptake (SERT). Cocaine: reuptake (DAT/NET/SERT). L-DOPA: synthesis (precursor bypassing tyrosine hydroxylase). Reserpine: storage (VMAT block). Donepezil: degradation (AChE). Diazepam: receptor (positive allosteric modulator of GABA_A). Haloperidol: receptor (D2 antagonist). Amphetamine: release (DAT reversal, VMAT2) and reuptake.
15. Massive glutamate overactivates NMDA receptors, so Ca²⁺ floods in. Calpains, phospholipases and NO synthase are activated, mitochondria fail, and ROS are produced, leading to cell death. NMDA receptors are also essential for normal function (learning, synaptic transmission). Memantine has a fast off-rate and blocks mainly during the prolonged, pathological activation, sparing brief physiological signals, so side effects are fewer than with complete blockers (which cause psychosis-like effects).
16. Fewer functional receptors mean a smaller end-plate potential with a reduced safety margin. With repeated stimulation, release declines (short-term depression), and the end-plate potential falls below threshold, so muscles fail (fatigue). AChE inhibitors (pyridostigmine) let ACh act longer and at higher concentration on the remaining receptors, restoring the safety margin.
17. **Early LTP** relies on modification of existing proteins (phosphorylation, AMPA trafficking) and needs no new protein. **Late LTP** and **long-term memory** need new gene transcription and protein synthesis (CREB, PRPs, structural changes). Blocking protein synthesis during this consolidation window prevents stabilisation.
