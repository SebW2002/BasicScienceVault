# Ch07 Organic Chemistry Essentials – Worksheet

> Theory: [[Ch07 Organic Chemistry Essentials]] · Solutions: [[Ch07 Organic Chemistry Essentials - Solutions]] · Log mistakes in [[Mistakes - Chemistry]]
> No calculator needed.

## Level 1 – Functional groups
1. Name the functional group: a) –COOH b) –NH₂ c) –SH d) –OPO₃²⁻ e) –OH f) –CO–NH–
2. Which charge do these groups carry at pH 7.4: carboxyl, primary amine, phosphate, hydroxyl?
3. Which functional groups does dopamine contain? (Structure: a benzene ring with two adjacent –OH groups and a –CH₂–CH₂–NH₂ side chain.)
4. Which functional group links acetate and choline in acetylcholine?

## Level 2 – Reactions
5. Is the formation of a peptide bond a condensation or a hydrolysis? What is released?
6. Acetylcholinesterase splits ACh. What type of reaction is this, and what are the products?
7. Two cysteine residues form a disulfide bridge. Is this an oxidation or a reduction?
8. Describe the conversion of glutamate to GABA. Which group is removed?
9. A protein is phosphorylated on a serine. Which group of serine is modified, and how does the charge of the protein change?

## Level 3 – Stereochemistry
10. What condition must a carbon atom fulfil to be a stereocentre?
11. Which amino acid is **not** chiral? Why?
12. What is the difference between cis/trans isomers and enantiomers?
13. Why can enantiomers have different effects in the body, although they have the same melting point and solubility?

## Level 4 – Neuro transfer
14. Acetylcholine does not cross the BBB, but nicotine does. Which structural difference explains this? (Hint: nicotine has a tertiary amine with p$K_a$ ≈ 8.)
15. Explain why esketamine and levodopa are given as single enantiomers.
16. Light switches retinal from 11-cis to all-trans. Why is a cis/trans isomerisation a good "light switch"?
17. Procaine (an ester) acts for a shorter time than lidocaine (an amide). Explain.
