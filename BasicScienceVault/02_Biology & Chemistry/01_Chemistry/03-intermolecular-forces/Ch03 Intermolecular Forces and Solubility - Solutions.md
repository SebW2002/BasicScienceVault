## Level 1
1. Hydrophilic: glucose, Na⁺. Hydrophobic: fatty acid tail, O₂, cholesterol (mostly; its single –OH makes it weakly amphiphilic). Amphiphilic: phospholipid, SDS.
2. Around K⁺, the oxygen atoms ($\delta^-$) point to the ion. Around Cl⁻, the hydrogen atoms ($\delta^+$) point to the ion.
3. a) micelles b) bilayers, which close into vesicles/liposomes

## Level 2
4. Water's dipoles form hydration shells around Na⁺ and Cl⁻ and shield their attraction (high $\varepsilon$). Oil has no dipoles that could stabilise the ions, so the crystal lattice stays intact.
5. Mg²⁺ carries **twice the charge** at a similar radius, so its charge density, and with it the attraction of the water dipoles, is much higher.
6. The hydrophobic effect: tails in contact with water force water into an ordered structure (low entropy). In the bilayer, tails touch only tails and heads touch water, which releases ordered water and increases entropy. The driving force is the free energy of the whole system.
7. $\log P = 3$: 1000 times more of the substance is in octanol than in water (very lipophilic). $\log P = -1$: ten times more is in water (hydrophilic).

10. Y: fewer polar groups give a higher $\log P$ and better BBB penetration. Disadvantages of extreme lipophilicity: poor water solubility (hard to formulate and dissolve in blood), strong binding to plasma proteins and fat tissue (less free drug), accumulation in membranes, and more nonspecific binding.

## Level 4
11. The acetyl groups mask two polar –OH groups, so heroin is more lipophilic and crosses the BBB faster. A higher concentration builds up in the brain in a short time (the "rush"). There, esterases remove the acetyl groups, producing 6-MAM and morphine, which act on µ-opioid receptors.
12. Dopamine is polar and does not cross the BBB. L-DOPA is an amino acid and is carried by the large neutral amino acid transporter (LAT1). In the brain, AADC converts it into dopamine. (Peripheral decarboxylase inhibitors such as carbidopa are added to stop conversion outside the brain.)
13. Antibodies are large hydrophilic proteins (about 150 kDa) and can't cross the lipid bilayer. The detergent dissolves membrane lipids, which creates pores and lets antibodies reach intracellular targets.
14. Ions can't move through the hydrophobic interior, and many stacked membrane layers greatly increase the electrical resistance and decrease the capacitance of the axon membrane. Less current leaks out and less charge is needed to change the voltage, which allows saltatory conduction.
