# Ch05 Redox and Electrochemistry – Solutions

> Worksheet: [[Ch05 Redox and Electrochemistry - Worksheet]] · Theory: [[Ch05 Redox and Electrochemistry]] · Wrong? → [[Mistakes - Chemistry]]

## Level 1
1. It is the combined driving force from the concentration difference and the electrical potential difference that determines the net movement of an ion across a membrane.
2. The electrical force exactly balances the diffusion force: ions still cross in both directions, but there is **no net flux**.
3. a) oxidation (electrons are released) b) reduction c) reduction
4. O₂ is the final acceptor. ATP synthase sits in the inner mitochondrial membrane.

## Level 2
5. $61.5\cdot\log_{10}10 = +61.5\,\text{mV}$
6. $61.5\cdot\log_{10}\frac{4}{160} = 61.5\cdot\log_{10}\frac1{40} = 61.5\cdot(-1.6)\approx-98\,\text{mV}$
7. $z = -1$: $E = -61.5\cdot\log_{10}\frac{120}{12} = -61.5\,\text{mV}$
8. $\frac{61.5}{2}\log_{10}\frac{2}{0.0002} = 30.75\cdot\log_{10}10^4 = 30.75\cdot4 = +123\,\text{mV}$
9. New value: $61.5\log_{10}\frac{40}{160} = 61.5\cdot(-0.6)\approx-37\,\text{mV}$. A tenfold rise in $c_o$ shifts $E_\text{K}$ by **+61.5 mV**, i.e. **depolarising**.

## Level 3
10. Na⁺: −70 − 60 = **−130 mV**, so it flows strongly inward (depolarising). K⁺: −70 − (−90) = **+20 mV**, so it flows outward (hyperpolarising). Cl⁻: −70 − (−70) = **0**, so there is no net flux.
11. No net current, because the driving force is zero. The synapse **still inhibits** through **shunting inhibition**: the open Cl⁻ channels raise the membrane conductance and "clamp" $V_m$ near −70 mV. Excitatory currents then produce smaller depolarisations (divisive effect, see [[A4 Factoring and Simplifying]]).
12. a) Numerator $= 4 + 0.06\cdot100 = 10$, denominator $= 100 + 0.06\cdot10 = 100.6\approx100$. $V_m\approx61.5\log_{10}0.1 = -61.5\,\text{mV}$.
    b) Numerator $= 4 + 10\cdot100 = 1004$, denominator $= 100 + 10\cdot10 = 200$, ratio ≈ 5. $V_m\approx61.5\cdot0.7\approx+43\,\text{mV}$, close to $E_\text{Na}$ (+61.5 mV).
13. $\Delta G\approx-2\cdot10^5\cdot1.14\approx-2.3\times10^5\,\text{J/mol} = -230\,\text{kJ/mol}$ (exact: −220). Theoretically about 4 ATP. In reality one NADH yields about 2.5 ATP; the rest is lost as heat.

## Level 4
14. Immature neurons express a lot of **NKCC1** (which imports Cl⁻) and little **KCC2** (which exports Cl⁻). High intracellular Cl⁻ makes $E_\text{Cl}$ more positive than the resting potential (e.g. −40 mV), so opening GABA_A channels lets Cl⁻ **flow out**, which depolarises the cell. When KCC2 is upregulated during development, internal Cl⁻ falls, $E_\text{Cl}$ becomes more negative, and GABA becomes inhibitory.
15. Higher $[\text{K}^+]_o$ makes $E_\text{K}$ more positive (Nernst). Because the resting potential is dominated by K⁺, $V_m$ also depolarises. This inactivates Na⁺ channels, opens Ca²⁺ channels and releases glutamate, leading to excitotoxicity and spreading depolarisation. Astrocytes normally **buffer K⁺** (via Kir4.1 channels and the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase) and distribute it through gap junctions.
16. (1) MPTP crosses the BBB and is converted by MAO-B in astrocytes into MPP⁺. (2) MPP⁺ is taken up selectively into dopaminergic neurons by the **dopamine transporter (DAT)**. (3) In mitochondria it inhibits **complex I**, so ATP falls, ROS rise, and the substantia nigra neurons die, causing parkinsonism.
17. Numerator: $1\cdot5 + 0.05\cdot145 + 0.45\cdot10 = 5 + 7.25 + 4.5 = 16.75$. Denominator: $1\cdot140 + 0.05\cdot15 + 0.45\cdot110 = 140 + 0.75 + 49.5 = 190.25$. Ratio $= 0.0880$, $\log_{10} = -1.056$, so $V_m\approx-64.9\,\text{mV}$.
