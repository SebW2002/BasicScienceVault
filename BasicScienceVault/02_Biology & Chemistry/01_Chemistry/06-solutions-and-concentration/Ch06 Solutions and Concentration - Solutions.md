## Level 3
8. a) The medium is hypotonic: water enters and the cell swells (and may burst). b) The medium is isotonic: no net change. c) The medium is hypertonic: water leaves and the cell shrinks.
9. 20 nm = 0.02 µm: $t = \frac{0.0004}{1} = 0.0004\,\text{ms} = 0.4\,\mu\text{s}$. For 1 µm: $t = \frac{1}{1} = 1\,\text{ms}$.
10. 1 cm = $10^4\,\mu\text{m}$: $t = \frac{10^8}{2}\,\text{ms} = 5\times10^7\,\text{ms} = 5\times10^4\,\text{s}\approx14\,\text{h}$. Over 1 m (100 times further), it is $10^4$ times longer, which is years even for small molecules and much longer for proteins. **Active axonal transport** (kinesin along microtubules, up to about 400 mm/day) is essential.
11. Twice the distance gives 4 times the time. Twice $D$ gives half the time.

## Level 4
12. Particles: NaCl $2\cdot125 = 250$; KCl $2\cdot2.5 = 5$; NaHCO₃ $2\cdot26 = 52$; NaH₂PO₄ $2\cdot1.25 = 2.5$; CaCl₂ $3\cdot2 = 6$; MgCl₂ $3\cdot1 = 3$; glucose 25. Sum ≈ **343.5 mOsm/L** (ideal). The measured value is noticeably lower (typically about 300–320 mOsm/kg), because ions in solution don't behave ideally (osmotic coefficient about 0.93). This is why lab solutions are checked with an **osmometer**.
13. Mannitol stays in the blood because it can't cross the intact BBB. It raises plasma osmolarity, so water is drawn **out** of brain tissue. If the BBB is broken, mannitol leaks into the tissue, the gradient disappears, and swelling can even get worse (rebound).
14. In chronic hyponatraemia, brain cells adapt by exporting osmolytes (e.g. glutamine, myo-inositol) to stop swelling. If plasma Na⁺ is raised quickly, the outside becomes relatively hypertonic and the cells, which now have few osmolytes, **shrink** rapidly. This damages oligodendrocytes and myelin, especially in the pons (**osmotic demyelination syndrome**).
15. $\pi = 1\cdot8.314\cdot310\approx2577\,\text{Pa}$, and $2577/133\approx19.4\,\text{mmHg}$. A gradient of only 1 mOsm/L produces as much pressure as a 26 cm column of water.
