# Ch02 Chemical Bonds – Solutions

> Worksheet: [[Ch02 Chemical Bonds - Worksheet]] · Theory: [[Ch02 Chemical Bonds]] · Wrong? → [[Mistakes - Chemistry]]

## Level 1
1. van der Waals < hydrogen bond < covalent C–C
2. A–T: 2. G–C: 3.
3. a) nonpolar covalent b) ionic c) coordinate (dative); sulfur donates the electron pair d) hydrogen bonds (plus base stacking)
4. From single to triple bonds, the bond gets shorter and stronger. Single bonds can rotate freely; double and triple bonds cannot (the π bonds would break).

## Level 2
5. CO₂ is **linear** (O=C=O), so the two bond dipoles point in opposite directions and cancel. H₂O is **bent** (≈ 104.5°), so the dipoles add up to a net dipole (the vector sum, see [[D1 Vectors in 2D and 3D]]).
6. In water, the high dielectric constant ($\varepsilon\approx80$) shields the charges. In addition, the water molecules form hydration shells that stabilise each ion on its own. The attraction is weakened about 80-fold.
7. Nonpolar molecules force the water around them into ordered cages, which costs entropy. When the nonpolar parts cluster, less water is ordered and the entropy of the system rises. It is **not** a real attraction between the nonpolar molecules; it is a water effect.
8. The C–N bond has partial double-bond character (resonance with C=O). As a result, six atoms lie in one plane, and only the bonds next to the Cα atom (the φ and ψ angles) can rotate. This restricts the possible folds.
9. In water, the O–H groups are donors and the lone pairs on O are acceptors (each water can donate 2 and accept 2 hydrogen bonds). In N–H···O=C, N–H is the donor and the carbonyl O is the acceptor.

## Level 3
10. The 60 % GC segment, because G–C pairs have 3 hydrogen bonds instead of 2 and stack more strongly. It has a higher melting temperature $T_m$.
11. A covalent bond (≈ 135 $RT$) is practically never broken by thermal motion. A hydrogen bond (≈ 8 $RT$) breaks occasionally, and a van der Waals contact (< $RT$) breaks all the time. Biology therefore uses covalent bonds for **stable structure** and weak bonds for **dynamic, reversible** processes such as binding, unbinding and conformational change.
12. a) $\frac{1}{2^2} = \frac14$: the force drops to a quarter. b) $\varepsilon$ falls by a factor of 20, so the force increases 20-fold. Salt bridges are strong inside proteins and weak on the surface.

## Level 4
13. Weak bonds allow **fast unbinding** (the signal ends within ms and the synapse is ready again). Their combination gives **specificity**: only a molecule with the right shape and charge pattern makes all the contacts. Affinity can also be **tuned** by small structural changes, which is useful for drug design.
14. Acetylcholinesterase is permanently blocked, so ACh accumulates in the cleft. Receptors are overstimulated and then desensitised. At muscles, this causes fasciculations followed by paralysis, including of the respiratory muscles. Muscarinic effects include salivation, miosis and bradycardia. Treatment includes atropine (muscarinic antagonist) and oximes (reactivate the enzyme before "aging").
15. Coordinate (ionic–dative) bonds: the phosphate oxygens donate electron pairs to Mg²⁺.
