# Ch01 Atomic Structure – Worksheet

> Theory: [[Ch01 Atomic Structure]] · Solutions: [[Ch01 Atomic Structure - Solutions]] · Log mistakes in [[Mistakes - Chemistry]]
> No calculator unless marked 🧮.

## Level 1 – Recall
1. Give the number of protons, neutrons and electrons in ²³Na, ³⁹K and ⁴⁰Ca²⁺.
2. Write the shell configuration (e.g. 2, 8, 1) for O, Cl and Ca.
3. How many valence electrons do C, N, Mg and Cl have?
4. Classify as cation or anion: Na⁺, Cl⁻, HCO₃⁻, Ca²⁺, NH₄⁺.

## Level 2 – Understanding
5. Explain why sodium forms Na⁺ and not Na²⁺, and why chlorine forms Cl⁻.
6. Order by increasing electronegativity: O, H, C, N, Na.
7. Classify the bond type from $\Delta EN$: a) C–H b) O–H c) Na–Cl d) C–C
8. Explain why a Na⁺ ion is smaller than a Na atom, and why Cl⁻ is larger than Cl.

## Level 3 – Calculation
9. How many moles of elementary charges correspond to $1\,\text{C}$? (Use $F\approx 10^5\,\text{C/mol}$.)
10. A current of $1\,\text{nA}$ carried by Na⁺ flows for $1\,\text{ms}$. How many Na⁺ ions is that? (Use $e\approx1.6\times10^{-19}\,\text{C}$.)
11. Ca²⁺ concentration is $10^{-4}\,\text{mM}$ inside and $1\,\text{mM}$ outside. What is the ratio outside/inside? What is $\log_{10}$ of this ratio?

## Level 4 – Neuro transfer
12. Which of the four key ions (Na⁺, K⁺, Cl⁻, Ca²⁺) is most concentrated a) inside a neuron, b) outside? Which has the largest concentration ratio?
13. K⁺ channels let K⁺ pass about 1000× more easily than Na⁺, although bare Na⁺ is smaller. Explain the principle.
14. Lithium is used as a mood stabiliser. Based on its position in the periodic table, which ion would you expect it to resemble chemically?
15. 🧮 A neuron with a membrane capacitance of $100\,\text{pF}$ depolarises by $100\,\text{mV}$ during an action potential. Charge $Q = CV$? How many Na⁺ ions must enter? Compare with the ~$10^{11}$ Na⁺ ions inside a typical cell – does the concentration change noticeably?
