# Ch01 Atomic Structure – Solutions

> Worksheet: [[Ch01 Atomic Structure - Worksheet]] · Theory: [[Ch01 Atomic Structure]] · Wrong? → [[Mistakes - Chemistry]]

## Level 1
1. ²³Na: 11 p, 12 n, 11 e⁻. ³⁹K: 19 p, 20 n, 19 e⁻. ⁴⁰Ca²⁺: 20 p, 20 n, **18** e⁻ (it has lost two).
2. O: 2, 6. Cl: 2, 8, 7. Ca: 2, 8, 8, 2.
3. C: 4. N: 5. Mg: 2. Cl: 7.
4. Cations: Na⁺, Ca²⁺, NH₄⁺. Anions: Cl⁻, HCO₃⁻.

## Level 2
5. Na has one valence electron. Losing it leaves the full shell configuration of neon (2, 8). Removing a second electron would break into a full shell and costs far too much energy. Cl has 7 valence electrons; gaining one completes the octet (argon configuration).
6. Na (0.93) < H (2.20) < C (2.55) < N (3.04) < O (3.44)
7. a) C–H: ΔEN = 0.35, nonpolar covalent. b) O–H: 1.24, polar covalent. c) Na–Cl: 2.23, ionic. d) C–C: 0, nonpolar covalent.
8. Na⁺ has lost its entire third shell, and the remaining electrons are pulled more strongly by the same 11 protons. Cl⁻ has one extra electron, which increases electron–electron repulsion at the same nuclear charge, so the electron cloud expands.

## Level 3
9. $n = Q/F = 1/10^5 = 10^{-5}\,\text{mol}$
10. $Q = I\cdot t = 10^{-9}\,\text{A}\cdot10^{-3}\,\text{s} = 10^{-12}\,\text{C}$. Number of ions $= \frac{10^{-12}}{1.6\times10^{-19}}\approx6\times10^{6}$ Na⁺ ions.
11. Ratio $= \frac{1}{10^{-4}} = 10^4$, and $\log_{10}10^4 = 4$.

## Level 4
12. a) K⁺ is most concentrated inside. b) Na⁺ is most concentrated outside (Cl⁻ is close). Ca²⁺ has the largest ratio, about $10^4$.
13. In water, ions carry a hydration shell. To enter the narrow selectivity filter, an ion must shed its water. The carbonyl oxygens of the filter are spaced so that they replace the water around K⁺ almost perfectly, which makes the "dehydration cost" small. Na⁺ is too small to touch all the oxygens at once, so it would lose its water without equivalent compensation. That makes entering energetically unfavourable for Na⁺.
14. Li is in group 1, so it forms Li⁺ like Na⁺ and K⁺. Because it is so small, its charge density also resembles Mg²⁺ (a "diagonal relationship"). It can compete for Mg²⁺ binding sites, for example in the enzymes inositol monophosphatase and GSK-3.
15. $Q = 100\times10^{-12}\,\text{F}\cdot0.1\,\text{V} = 10^{-11}\,\text{C}$. Ions: $\frac{10^{-11}}{1.6\times10^{-19}}\approx6.25\times10^{7}$. Relative to about $10^{11}$ Na⁺ ions already inside, that is roughly $6\times10^{-4}$, i.e. under 0.1 %. **A single action potential barely changes the concentrations.** It is only the redistribution of a tiny amount of charge at the membrane that changes the voltage.
