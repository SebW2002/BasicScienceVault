# Ch08 Biomolecules – Solutions

> Worksheet: [[Ch08 Biomolecules - Worksheet]] · Theory: [[Ch08 Biomolecules]] · Wrong? → [[Mistakes - Chemistry]]

## Level 1
1. Proteins (e.g. Na⁺ channels), lipids (e.g. sphingomyelin in myelin), carbohydrates (e.g. glucose as fuel), nucleic acids (e.g. DNA; ATP as a nucleotide).
2. Base + sugar + phosphate(s). DNA nucleotides contain deoxyribose (no 2'-OH) and the base T; RNA nucleotides contain ribose and the base U.
3. Negative: Asp, Glu. Positive: Lys, Arg (His partly).
4. Amino acids as transmitters: glutamate, glycine, aspartate (D-serine as a co-agonist). Derived: dopamine/noradrenaline (from tyrosine), serotonin (from tryptophan), histamine (from histidine), GABA (from glutamate).

## Level 2
5. The –COOH of amino acid 1 reacts with the –NH₂ of amino acid 2, releasing H₂O and forming –CO–NH–. The lone pair on N is delocalised into the C=O bond (resonance), giving the C–N bond partial double-bond character, so it is planar and can't rotate.
6. Saturated fatty acids have only single bonds and straight tails that pack tightly, so membranes are less fluid. Unsaturated fatty acids have cis double bonds, which put kinks in the tails, giving looser packing and a **more fluid** membrane.
7. Within a strand: **phosphodiester bonds** (covalent). Between strands: **hydrogen bonds** between complementary bases (plus stacking interactions).
8. N-linked: on the amide N of **Asn**, starting co-translationally in the **ER**. O-linked: on the –OH of **Ser/Thr**, mainly in the **Golgi**.

## Level 3
9. $20^3 = 8000$ tripeptides; $4^{10} = 1\,048\,576$ DNA sequences.
10. Write the complement directly under the template. The strands are antiparallel:
    ```
    5'-A T G C C G T A-3'
    3'-T A C G G C A T-5'
    ```
    Read 5'→3' (the usual convention), the complement is the reverse: **5'-TACGGCAT-3'**. (Coincidentally, this sequence reads the same in both directions, so writing it the wrong way round wouldn't show here. With other sequences, always check the direction!)

    Pairs: A–T, T–A, G–C, C–G, C–G, G–C, T–A, A–T, which is 4 AT (2 H-bonds each) + 4 GC (3 each) = 8 + 12 = **20 hydrogen bonds**.
11. The products (ADP + Pᵢ) are more stable than ATP: there is less charge repulsion between phosphates, more resonance stabilisation of Pᵢ, and better hydration. In addition, the cell keeps ATP far above equilibrium, so its hydrolysis is strongly exergonic. Consumers: the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase, Ca²⁺ pumps (PMCA, SERCA), the V-ATPase for vesicle filling, axonal transport (kinesin/dynein), and synthesis of transmitters and proteins.

## Level 4
12. Lipoproteins carrying cholesterol from the blood can't cross the BBB, so neurons and especially astrocytes and oligodendrocytes (for myelin) synthesise it themselves. **APOE**: apolipoprotein E transports cholesterol and lipids between brain cells. The **ε4** allele increases Alzheimer's risk, among other ways by affecting amyloid-β clearance and aggregation.
13. Adenosine (from ATP breakdown) accumulates during wakefulness and promotes sleep pressure through A1 and A2A receptors. Caffeine is an **antagonist** at these receptors, so the sleep-promoting signal is blocked.
14. **Tyrosine** becomes deficient (unless it is supplied in the diet), which affects **catecholamine** synthesis (dopamine, noradrenaline). In addition, high phenylalanine competes with other large neutral amino acids (including tyrosine and tryptophan) for LAT1 transport into the brain, so serotonin synthesis can also be reduced. Untreated, PKU leads to intellectual disability.
15. The glycosylated extracellular matrix actively **stabilises** mature circuits and acts as a "brake" on plasticity. Carbohydrate-rich structures are not just packaging but regulators of neuronal function.
