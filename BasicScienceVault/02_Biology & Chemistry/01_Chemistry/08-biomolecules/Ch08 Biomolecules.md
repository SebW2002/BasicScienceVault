# Ch08 Biomolecules

> [[MOC-chemistry|↑ Chemistry MOC]] · [[Ch08 Biomolecules - Worksheet|✏️ Worksheet]] · [[Ch08 Biomolecules - Solutions|✅ Solutions]] · [[Mistakes - Chemistry|⚠️ Mistake log]]

## Why this matters
Four classes of molecules build every neuron: **proteins** (channels, receptors, enzymes), **lipids** (membranes, myelin, signalling), **carbohydrates** (energy, glycosylation) and **nucleic acids** (genes, plus ATP and cAMP as energy and messenger molecules). This note is the bridge from chemistry to biology.

## 1 · Amino acids and peptide bonds
**General structure:** a central α-carbon carrying an amino group (–NH₃⁺), a carboxyl group (–COO⁻), an H and a **side chain R**. At pH 7.4 it is a **zwitterion** (both charges at once).
- 20 standard amino acids. All except glycine are chiral, and proteins use the **L** form ([[Ch07 Organic Chemistry Essentials]]).

| Group | Amino acids (3-letter / 1-letter) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Nonpolar / hydrophobic | Gly G, Ala A, Val V, Leu L, Ile I, Met M, Phe F, Trp W, Pro P | protein cores, transmembrane helices; Pro breaks helices |
| Polar, uncharged | Ser S, Thr T, Cys C, Asn N, Gln Q, Tyr Y | Ser/Thr/Tyr can be phosphorylated; Cys forms disulfides; Asn/Ser/Thr are glycosylated |
| Acidic (−) | Asp D, Glu E | Glu is itself a neurotransmitter |
| Basic (+) | Lys K, Arg R, His H (partly) | Arg/Lys in the S4 voltage sensor of channels |

**Peptide bond:** the carboxyl of one amino acid and the amino group of the next join by condensation (releasing H₂O) to form an amide bond. It is **planar** and rigid (partial double bond) and is written in the direction **N-terminus → C-terminus**.
- Peptide (< ~50 aa) vs. protein; structure levels are covered in [[Bio03 Proteins and Enzymes]].
- **Neuropeptides:** oxytocin and vasopressin (9 aa), substance P (11 aa), enkephalins (5 aa), β-endorphin (31 aa), neuropeptide Y (36 aa).
- **Amino acids as transmitters or precursors:**
  - glutamate, glycine, aspartate and D-serine act directly
  - tyrosine is the precursor of catecholamines; tryptophan of serotonin and melatonin; histidine of histamine; glutamate of GABA

## 2 · Carbohydrates
- **Monosaccharides:** glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆), fructose, galactose, ribose (C₅).
- **Glycosidic bonds** link sugars by condensation: disaccharides (sucrose, lactose) and polysaccharides (glycogen in animals, mostly in astrocytes in the brain; starch and cellulose in plants).
- **Glucose** is the main fuel of the brain (≈ 120 g/day). It crosses the BBB via **GLUT1** and enters neurons via **GLUT3**.
- **Glycosylation:** sugar chains are attached to proteins and lipids (glycoproteins, glycolipids, proteoglycans).
  - **N-linked** (on Asn) starts in the ER; **O-linked** (on Ser/Thr) happens mainly in the Golgi ([[Bio01 Cell Biology]]).
  - Functions: correct folding and quality control, transport to the membrane, cell–cell recognition and adhesion, and channel gating.
  - Neuro examples:
    - **Polysialic acid on NCAM** regulates plasticity and migration.
    - The **perineuronal nets** around PV interneurons (chondroitin sulfate proteoglycans) close critical periods.
    - Defects in glycosylation (congenital disorders of glycosylation) often cause severe neurological symptoms.

## 3 · Lipids
Lipids are hydrophobic or amphiphilic molecules that are poorly soluble in water ([[Ch03 Intermolecular Forces and Solubility]]).

| Lipid | Structure | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Fatty acids** | long hydrocarbon chain + –COOH; saturated (straight) or unsaturated (cis kinks) | energy, building blocks; DHA (an ω-3 fatty acid) is enriched in neuronal membranes |
| Triglycerides | glycerol + 3 fatty acids | energy store (little in the brain) |
| **Phospholipids** | glycerol + 2 fatty acids + phosphate + head group (choline → PC, ethanolamine → PE, serine → PS, inositol → PI) | membrane bilayer; **PIP₂** is a signalling lipid (→ IP₃ + DAG) and regulates many channels |
| **Sphingolipids** | sphingosine backbone; sphingomyelin, gangliosides, galactocerebroside | **myelin**, lipid rafts; ganglioside storage diseases (Tay–Sachs) |
| **Cholesterol** | rigid four-ring steroid + one –OH | membrane fluidity buffer, myelin, precursor of steroid hormones and neurosteroids |
| Endocannabinoids | arachidonic-acid derivatives (anandamide, 2-AG) | retrograde messengers at synapses (CB1 receptor) |

- **Myelin** is about 70–80 % lipid by dry weight (cholesterol, galactocerebroside, sphingomyelin), which makes it an excellent insulator.
- The brain contains about 20–25 % of the body's cholesterol while making up only about 2 % of body mass. Brain cholesterol is **made locally**, because lipoprotein-bound cholesterol from the blood does not cross the BBB. **ApoE** transports lipids in the brain, and the **APOE4** allele is the strongest common genetic risk factor for late-onset Alzheimer's disease.

## 4 · Nucleotides and nucleic acids
**Nucleotide** = nitrogenous **base** + **sugar** (ribose in RNA, deoxyribose in DNA) + 1–3 **phosphates**.
- Purines (2 rings): adenine A, guanine G. Pyrimidines (1 ring): cytosine C, thymine T (DNA), uracil U (RNA).
- **Nucleic acids:** nucleotides are linked by **phosphodiester bonds** (sugar–phosphate backbone, directional 5' → 3', negatively charged).
- **DNA:** a double helix of two **antiparallel** strands with complementary base pairs, A–T (2 H-bonds) and G–C (3 H-bonds) ([[Ch02 Chemical Bonds]]).
- **RNA:** single-stranded and can fold. Types include mRNA, tRNA, rRNA and many regulatory RNAs (miRNA, lncRNA). See [[Bio04 Nucleic Acids and Gene Expression]].

**Nucleotides beyond genes**
- **ATP:** the universal energy currency. Hydrolysis of the terminal phosphoanhydride bond releases about 30.5 kJ/mol under standard conditions (about 50 kJ/mol in cells). ATP is also a co-transmitter, and its breakdown product adenosine accumulates during wakefulness and promotes sleep (blocked by caffeine).
- **GTP:** powers G proteins and microtubule dynamics.
- **cAMP and cGMP:** second messengers (GPCRs → adenylyl cyclase → cAMP → PKA; NO → guanylyl cyclase → cGMP). See [[Bio03 Proteins and Enzymes]].
- **NAD⁺/NADH, FAD/FADH₂:** electron carriers in metabolism ([[Ch05 Redox and Electrochemistry]]).

## In neuroscience #neuro
- **One molecule, many roles:** glutamate is an amino acid (protein building block), a metabolite, the main excitatory transmitter, and the precursor of GABA.
- **Lipid membranes set electrical properties:** the bilayer gives a specific capacitance of about 1 µF/cm². Myelin's many layers reduce capacitance, which is the basis of fast conduction ([[Bio05 Neurons and Action Potentials]]).
- **Clinical:**
  - **PKU** (phenylketonuria): phenylalanine can't be converted to tyrosine, which damages the brain without a diet low in Phe.
  - Lysosomal **sphingolipid** storage diseases (Tay–Sachs, Niemann–Pick) cause neurodegeneration.
  - **Repeat expansions** in DNA (CAG in Huntington's disease) produce polyglutamine proteins.

## Key takeaways
- Amino acids are zwitterions; peptide bonds are planar amides joined by condensation, written N → C.
- Glycosylation tags proteins for folding, sorting and recognition (ER/Golgi).
- Phospholipids build membranes; cholesterol and sphingolipids build myelin; the brain makes its own cholesterol.
- Nucleotides are genes (DNA/RNA) **and** energy (ATP) **and** messengers (cAMP).
