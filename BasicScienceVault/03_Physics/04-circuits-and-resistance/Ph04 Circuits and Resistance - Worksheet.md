# Ph04 Circuits and Resistance – Worksheet

> Theory: [[Ph04 Circuits and Resistance]] · Solutions: [[Ph04 Circuits and Resistance - Solutions]] · Log mistakes in [[Mistakes - Physics]]
> No calculator needed.

## Level 1 – Ohm's law
1. A 100 Ω resistor carries 0.2 A. What is the voltage across it?
2. A neuron has an input resistance of 200 MΩ. Injecting 50 pA changes its voltage by how much?
3. Convert: 20 pS to Ω; 100 MΩ to nS.
4. A channel has a conductance of 10 pS and a driving force of −80 mV. Compute the current.

## Level 2 – Series and parallel
5. Two resistors of 100 Ω and 300 Ω: total resistance in series? In parallel?
6. 1000 identical channels of 10 pS each are open in parallel. Total conductance and resistance?
7. Three membrane patches of 300, 300 and 150 MΩ are in parallel. Total resistance?
8. A whole-cell recording has a series resistance of 10 MΩ. The cell draws 1 nA. How large is the voltage error? What does this mean for a voltage-clamp experiment?

## Level 3 – Kirchhoff and the equivalent circuit
9. Three currents enter a node: 2 nA, −0.5 nA and an unknown $I_3$. What must $I_3$ be (KCL)?
10. A membrane has $g_\text{K} = 10\,\text{nS}$ ($E_\text{K} = -90$ mV) and $g_\text{Na} = 1\,\text{nS}$ ($E_\text{Na} = +60$ mV). Derive and compute the resting potential.
11. In task 10, a synapse opens with $g_\text{syn} = 4\,\text{nS}$ and $E_\text{syn} = 0$ mV. New steady-state potential?
12. Instead, an inhibitory synapse with $g = 5\,\text{nS}$ and $E = -76$ mV opens (task 10 values). New potential? Compute the input resistance before and after.

## Level 4 – Neuro transfer
13. Explain with Ohm's law why small motor neurons fire before large ones when they receive the same synaptic current (size principle).
14. Why does opening many inhibitory channels with $E\approx V_\text{rest}$ make the neuron less excitable although $V_\text{rest}$ doesn't change?
15. Draw the equivalent circuit of two neurons coupled by a gap junction. Which element represents the gap junction?
