# Ph12 Quantum Basics for Biophysics – Solutions

> Worksheet: [[Ph12 Quantum Basics for Biophysics - Worksheet]] · Theory: [[Ph12 Quantum Basics for Biophysics]] · Wrong? → [[Mistakes - Physics]]

## Level 1
1. 620 nm: 2 eV. 496 nm: 2.5 eV. 1240 nm: 1 eV.
2. Blue: $650/450\approx1.44$ times more energy (2.76 eV vs. 1.91 eV).
3. $1240/470\approx2.64\,\text{eV}$, so $2.64\cdot96.5\approx255\,\text{kJ/mol}$, and $2.64/0.027\approx98\,kT$.
4. UV photons (≈ 4 eV and more) have enough energy to excite DNA bases directly and drive photochemical reactions (thymine dimers). A single red photon (≈ 2 eV) can't, and the energies of separate photons don't add up (except in simultaneous multi-photon absorption at very high intensities).

## Level 2
5. Each photon must carry half the energy, i.e. twice the wavelength: 920 nm.
6. $(1/3)^2 = 1/9$: the signal drops ninefold.
7. Noise $= \sqrt{100} = 10$; SNR $= 100/10 = 10$. For SNR = 30: $N = 900$ photons.
8. Change: 2 % of 10,000 = 200 photons. Shot noise: $\sqrt{10\,000} = 100$. The change is twice the noise (SNR ≈ 2): barely detectable in a single frame. Averaging or more photons are needed, which is the "photon budget" problem of voltage imaging.

## Level 3
9. Nuclear spin (~10⁻⁷ eV) < vibrational (~0.1 eV) < electronic (~1–10 eV).
10. Delocalised electrons in a longer conjugated system behave like particles in a larger "box". Energy levels are closer together ($E\propto1/L^2$), so the gap between the highest occupied and lowest unoccupied levels is smaller. A smaller energy gap means a longer absorbed wavelength.
11. The amino acids surrounding retinal (charges, dipoles, hydrogen bonds near the chromophore and its protonated Schiff base) shift the energies of the ground and excited states differently, which **tunes** the energy gap. This "spectral tuning" gives the different colour sensitivities of the cones and can be engineered into opsins.
12. $I/I_0 = 10^{-0.5}\approx0.32$: about 32 % passes through (68 % is absorbed).

## Level 4
13. Tunnelling probability decreases exponentially with $\sqrt{m}$ times the distance. Electrons are about 70,000 times lighter than K⁺ ions, so they tunnel across 1–2 nm with relevant probability. For a K⁺ ion, the exponent is hundreds of times larger, which makes the probability effectively zero. K⁺ ions cross channels classically: thermally driven hopping between binding sites.
14. 5 Å extra ≈ 3 × 1.7 Å, so a factor of about $10^3$ lower. Cofactors must therefore be positioned very precisely and closely (≤ ~14 Å) to allow fast electron transfer, and the protein structure determines the electron path.
15. (1) **Less scattering** at longer wavelengths (scattering decreases strongly with λ). (2) **Less absorption** by haemoglobin, which absorbs blue and green strongly but red and NIR weakly. (Further: lower phototoxicity per photon.)
16. $E = \frac{6.63\times10^{-34}\cdot3\times10^8}{470\times10^{-9}}\approx4.23\times10^{-19}\,\text{J}$; photons per second $= \frac{10^{-3}}{4.23\times10^{-19}}\approx2.4\times10^{15}$.
