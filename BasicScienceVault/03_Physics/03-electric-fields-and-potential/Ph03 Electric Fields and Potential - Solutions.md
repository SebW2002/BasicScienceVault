# Ph03 Electric Fields and Potential – Solutions

> Worksheet: [[Ph03 Electric Fields and Potential - Worksheet]] · Theory: [[Ph03 Electric Fields and Potential]] · Wrong? → [[Mistakes - Physics]]

## Level 1
1. The electric field is the force per unit charge acting on a (small) test charge: $\vec E = \vec F/q$. Units: N/C = V/m.
2. $E = \frac{0.01}{2\times10^{-6}} = 5000\,\text{N/C}$
3. The **potential** is the energy per charge at one point relative to an arbitrarily chosen reference (e.g. ground). The **potential difference** (voltage) is the difference between two points. Only differences are physically measurable.
4. Positive ions move in the direction of the field (towards lower potential); negative ions move against it.

## Level 2
5. $E = \frac{10}{0.02} = 500\,\text{V/m}$
6. $E = \frac{0.07}{5\times10^{-9}} = 1.4\times10^7\,\text{V/m} = 14\,\text{MV/m}$, about five times the breakdown field of air.
7. $W = e\cdot1\,\text{V} = 1.6\times10^{-19}\,\text{J} = 1\,\text{eV}$
8. $W = e\cdot0.07 = 1.12\times10^{-20}\,\text{J}\approx2.6\,kT$ (released, because the positive ion moves towards the negative inside).

## Level 3
9. (1) K⁺ diffuses out down its concentration gradient. (2) Each ion takes positive charge with it, so the inside becomes negative. (3) The electric field pulls K⁺ back in. (4) At $E_\text{K}$, the outward diffusion flux and the inward electrical flux are equal, so there is no further net change.
10. Ions keep crossing randomly in both directions (thermal motion). At $E_\text{K}$ the **rates** in both directions are equal, so the **net** flux is zero (dynamic equilibrium).
11. Electrical: $96\,500\cdot0.09\approx8.7\,\text{kJ/mol}$. Chemical: $2.6\cdot\ln(1/30) = -2.6\cdot3.4\approx-8.8\,\text{kJ/mol}$. They are equal in magnitude and opposite in sign, which is exactly the meaning of the equilibrium potential: $E_\text{K}\approx-90$ mV for a 30-fold gradient.
12. The membrane capacitance is small, so very little charge is needed for a voltage of 70 mV. For a typical cell, this is a fraction of about $10^{-4}$–$10^{-5}$ of the ions in the cell ([[Ch01 Atomic Structure - Solutions]], task 15).

## Level 4
13. Energy $= 3e\cdot0.5\cdot0.07\,\text{V}$: per elementary charge $0.035\,\text{eV}$, and $0.035/0.0267\approx1.3\,kT$; for 3 charges, $\approx3.9\,kT$. According to the Boltzmann distribution ([[Ph08 Thermodynamics and Statistics]]), the ratio of open to closed probability then changes by about $e^{3.9}\approx50$-fold. The more gating charge, the **steeper** the voltage dependence (real Kv channels move ~12–13 $e$ in total).
14. $\Delta V = E\cdot d = 0.5\cdot10^{-3} = 0.5\,\text{mV}$. That's far below threshold, but (1) it acts on **millions** of neurons simultaneously and coherently, (2) it shifts **timing** and spike probability of neurons near threshold, (3) it can modulate plasticity when combined with ongoing activity.
15. $V = \frac{9\times10^9\cdot1.6\times10^{-19}}{80\cdot10^{-9}} = \frac{1.44\times10^{-9}}{8\times10^{-8}} = 0.018\,\text{V} = 18\,\text{mV}$. That's comparable to $kT/e$. At around 1 nm in water, single charges have electrostatic effects on the order of thermal energy, which is the length scale (the Bjerrum length, ≈ 0.7 nm) on which charged groups in proteins and lipids "feel" each other.
