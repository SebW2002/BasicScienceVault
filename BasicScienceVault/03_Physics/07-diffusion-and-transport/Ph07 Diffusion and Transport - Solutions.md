# Ph07 Diffusion and Transport – Solutions

> Worksheet: [[Ph07 Diffusion and Transport - Worksheet]] · Theory: [[Ph07 Diffusion and Transport]] · Wrong? → [[Mistakes - Physics]]

## Level 1
1. Every molecule is equally likely to move left or right. But there are more molecules on the high-concentration side, so more molecules cross from high to low than in the other direction. The net flux is a statistical consequence, and the system moves towards the most probable (mixed) state, which is an increase in entropy.
2. $J = -D\frac{\partial c}{\partial x}$. The gradient points towards **increasing** concentration; flux goes in the opposite direction (downhill), hence the minus sign.
3. a) Increases by $310/300\approx1.033$, about 3 % (plus viscosity falls slightly with temperature, so in reality a bit more). b) Halves. c) Falls to one-third.
4. Net diffusion of water through a semipermeable membrane. Water flows towards the side with **higher solute concentration** (higher osmolarity).

## Level 2
5. 1 µm: 0.5 ms. 10 µm: 50 ms. 100 µm: 5000 ms = 5 s.
6. 1 s = 1000 ms: $x = \sqrt{2\cdot0.1\cdot1000} = \sqrt{200}\approx14\,\mu\text{m}$. 1 min = 60,000 ms: $x = \sqrt{12\,000}\approx110\,\mu\text{m}$.
7. $\frac{dc}{dx} = \frac{-10\,\text{mol/m}^3}{10^{-5}\,\text{m}} = -10^6\,\text{mol/m}^4$, so $J = -10^{-9}\cdot(-10^6) = 10^{-3}\,\text{mol/(m}^2\text{s)}$, in the direction of decreasing concentration.
8. $\sigma\propto\sqrt t$, so $\sqrt4 = 2$: twice as wide. The 1D peak $\propto1/\sqrt t$ falls to half (the total amount is conserved: twice as wide means half as high).

## Level 3
9. The first term is **diffusion** (driven by the concentration gradient, Fick). The second term is **electrical drift** of charged particles in the field. If $J = 0$, the two terms cancel, and integrating gives the **Nernst equation**, the voltage at which the concentration gradient is in equilibrium.
10. The drift flux is the number of ions moved per area per time. It is proportional to how many ions are present (concentration) times their drift velocity in the field, which is proportional to the field strength. Double the ions, double the electrical flux.
11. $c = 300\,\text{mol/m}^3$: $\pi = 300\cdot8.3\cdot310\approx7.7\times10^5\,\text{Pa}\approx7.7\,\text{atm}$. That's why cells must be in an isotonic environment: unbalanced, this pressure would burst them.
12. $\lambda_t^2 = 2.56$: effective diffusion is about 2.6 times slower.

## Level 4
13. Ca²⁺ entering through a channel is quickly captured by buffers (calbindin, parvalbumin, ATP) and pumped away. The free Ca²⁺ concentration is high (≈ 10–100 µM) only in a **nanodomain** of a few tens of nm around the channel mouth. Synaptotagmin has a low affinity and needs this high local concentration, so release is fast and tightly coupled only if channel and sensor are very close. Fast buffers (BAPTA) can intercept Ca²⁺ even over short distances; slow buffers (EGTA) only over longer ones, and this difference is used to estimate the coupling distance.
14. Cytotoxic oedema: Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase failure makes cells swell, the extracellular space shrinks and becomes more tortuous, and water mobility is restricted, so the ADC falls within minutes. The infarct core is visible in diffusion-weighted MRI long before it becomes visible on CT or T2-weighted MRI, which guides the decision for thrombolysis or thrombectomy.
15. $D = \frac{4.3\times10^{-21}}{6\pi\cdot0.7\times10^{-3}\cdot0.4\times10^{-9}} = \frac{4.3\times10^{-21}}{5.28\times10^{-12}}\approx8.1\times10^{-10}\,\text{m}^2/\text{s}\approx0.8\,\mu\text{m}^2/\text{ms}$ (measured: ≈ 0.7–0.9 µm²/ms at body temperature).
