## Level 1

## Level 3
9. $K = e^{11.8/2.6}$; since $11.8 = 2\cdot5.9$, $K = 10^2 = 100$.
10. $\Delta G^\circ = RT\ln K_d = RT\cdot\ln10^{-9} = -9\cdot5.9\approx-53\,\text{kJ/mol}$ (about 20 $RT$).
11. $zFV_m = -RT\ln\frac{c_\text{in}}{c_\text{out}} = RT\ln\frac{c_\text{out}}{c_\text{in}}$, so $V_m = E = \frac{RT}{zF}\ln\frac{c_\text{out}}{c_\text{in}}$ ✓
12. Chemical: $2.6\cdot\ln0.1 = 2.6\cdot(-2.3)\approx-6.0\,\text{kJ/mol}$. Electrical: $1\cdot96.5\cdot(-0.07)\approx-6.8\,\text{kJ/mol}$. Total ≈ **−12.8 kJ/mol** < 0: spontaneous. Both forces push Na⁺ in, which is why opening Na⁺ channels produces a strong inward current.

## Level 4
13. "All left": 1 arrangement. "2 left, 2 right": $\binom42 = 6$. Of the $2^4 = 16$ possible arrangements, the even distribution is the most likely. With $10^{20}$ molecules, the probability of significant deviations is practically zero, so diffusion towards even distribution is "inevitable".
14. Gradients are low-entropy states. Ions diffuse through leak channels and during signalling, so the gradients would run down (towards equilibrium, maximum entropy). The **Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase** (and other pumps) maintains them with ATP, i.e. by consuming free energy from metabolism.
15. Vesicle fusion is a thermally activated process across an energy barrier, so whether it happens in a given ms is random (Boltzmann/Arrhenius). Ca²⁺ binds cooperatively to several sites on synaptotagmin; each bound Ca²⁺ lowers the barrier further. Because the rate depends **exponentially** on the barrier, and several Ca²⁺ are needed, release rises very steeply (about with the 4th power of [Ca²⁺]).
16. Na⁺ out (3×): per mol $\Delta G = RT\ln\frac{145}{15} + F\cdot(+0.07)$ (moving + charge from −70 to 0 mV costs energy) $= 2.58\cdot2.27 + 96.5\cdot0.07\approx5.9 + 6.8 = 12.6$ kJ/mol, so ×3 ≈ **37.8 kJ**.
    K⁺ in (2×): $RT\ln\frac{140}{5} + F\cdot(-0.07) = 2.58\cdot3.33 - 6.8\approx8.6 - 6.8 = 1.8$ kJ/mol, so ×2 ≈ **3.6 kJ**.
    Total ≈ **41 kJ per mol of cycles** < 50 kJ from ATP, so it is feasible (efficiency ≈ 80 %). This shows why the pump moves exactly this stoichiometry: much more cargo per ATP would no longer be thermodynamically possible.
