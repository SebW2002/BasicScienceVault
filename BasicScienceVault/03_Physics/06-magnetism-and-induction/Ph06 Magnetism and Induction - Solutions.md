# Ph06 Magnetism and Induction – Solutions

> Worksheet: [[Ph06 Magnetism and Induction - Worksheet]] · Theory: [[Ph06 Magnetism and Induction]] · Wrong? → [[Mistakes - Physics]]

## Level 1
1. **Moving charges (currents)** create magnetic fields (as do permanent magnets, through the spins of electrons). A **changing** magnetic field induces an electric field (voltage).
2. MEG signal (fT) < Earth's field (50 µT) < fridge magnet (mT) < TMS pulse (1–2 T) ≈ MRI scanner (1.5–3 T).
3. Counter-clockwise (thumb up, fingers curl counter-clockwise when viewed from above).
4. The induced voltage in a loop equals the negative rate of change of magnetic flux through it. No: only **changes** in flux induce a voltage.

## Level 2
5. $B = \frac{4\pi\times10^{-7}\cdot10}{2\pi\cdot0.01} = \frac{2\times10^{-7}\cdot10}{0.01} = 2\times10^{-4}\,\text{T} = 200\,\mu\text{T}$, four times the Earth's field.
6. $\mathcal E = A\frac{\Delta B}{\Delta t} = 0.01\cdot\frac{1}{10^{-4}} = 100\,\text{V}$
7. $E = 0.01\cdot10^4 = 100\,\text{V/m}$
8. $B = \frac{2\times10^{-7}\cdot10^{-9}}{0.01} = 2\times10^{-14}\,\text{T} = 20\,\text{fT}$. That is in the range of MEG signals (which is why MEG sees summed currents of many neurons). It is $2.5\times10^9$ times smaller than the Earth's field.

## Level 3
9. The skull has a **high electrical resistance**, so injected currents mainly flow through the scalp (stimulating pain receptors) and are spread out. Magnetic fields pass bone and tissue **without attenuation** and induce the electric field directly in the brain.
10. The currents in the two adjacent wings flow in **opposite directions**, so their induced fields **add** below the centre where the wings meet and partly cancel elsewhere. The result is a focal maximum.
11. The magnetic field of a dipole points perpendicular to the current direction ($\propto\vec Q\times\vec r$). For a radial dipole in a sphere, the volume currents and the primary current produce fields that cancel outside. MEG sees **tangential** sources best: those in **sulcal walls** (e.g. auditory cortex on the supratemporal plane, somatosensory cortex in the central sulcus).
12. Induction depends on $dB/dt$, not $B$. The static field doesn't change, so it induces nothing. Gradients are smaller but switch in µs–ms (large $dB/dt$), inducing electric fields in the body that can excite peripheral nerves.

## Level 4
13. Capacitor discharge → large, brief coil current → rapidly changing magnetic field → (Faraday) induced electric field in left M1 → depolarisation of axons of cortical neurons (mainly interneurons and pyramidal cell axons) → APs in layer 5 corticospinal neurons → descending volleys via the pyramidal tract (crossing in the medulla) → spinal α-motor neurons → muscle → twitch in the right hand (the MEP, measured by EMG).
14. Low-frequency stimulation resembles LTD induction protocols (moderate Ca²⁺, phosphatases, AMPA receptor removal). High-frequency or theta-burst stimulation resembles LTP protocols (strong NMDA activation, CaMKII, AMPA insertion). Effects are NMDA-dependent (blocked by NMDA antagonists in human studies), which supports this interpretation.
15. $B\approx10^{-7}\cdot\frac{2\times10^{-8}}{(0.04)^2} = 10^{-7}\cdot\frac{2\times10^{-8}}{1.6\times10^{-3}}\approx1.25\times10^{-12}\,\text{T}$ = 1250 fT. (A rough upper estimate; real measured fields are often 100–1000 fT because of geometry and cancellation.)
