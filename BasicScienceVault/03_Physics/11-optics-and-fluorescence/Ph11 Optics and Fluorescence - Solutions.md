# Ph11 Optics and Fluorescence – Solutions

> Worksheet: [[Ph11 Optics and Fluorescence - Worksheet]] · Theory: [[Ph11 Optics and Fluorescence]] · Wrong? → [[Mistakes - Physics]]

## Level 1
1. $n = \frac{3\times10^8}{2.25\times10^8} = 1.33$
2. Towards the normal (into the denser medium). $\sin\theta_2 = \frac{1\cdot0.5}{1.33}\approx0.376$ ($\theta_2\approx22°$).
3. $\sin\theta_c = \frac{1}{1.5}\approx0.67\Rightarrow\theta_c\approx42°$
4. Its many components (lipid-rich membranes and myelin, organelles, cytoplasm) have **different refractive indices**. Light is scattered many times at these interfaces, so the tissue looks white and opaque, like milk or snow. Tissue clearing works by equalising $n$.

## Level 2
5. $\frac{1}{d_i} = \frac12 - \frac13 = \frac16\Rightarrow d_i = 6\,\text{cm}$; $M = -6/3 = -2$: real, inverted, twice the size.
6. $\frac1{d_i} = \frac12 - \frac14 = \frac14\Rightarrow d_i = 4\,\text{cm}$; $M = -1$ (at $2f$: same size, inverted).
7. 600×. No: resolution is set by the objective's NA (and λ). A stronger eyepiece only enlarges the blurred image ("empty magnification").
8. $D = 1/0.5\,\text{m} = 2\,\text{dpt}$

## Level 3
9. $d = \frac{600}{2.4} = 250\,\text{nm}$; with NA 0.8, $d = \frac{600}{1.6} = 375\,\text{nm}$.
10. Diffraction blurs on the scale of the wavelength, so smaller λ gives smaller blur. Electrons accelerated at 100–300 kV have de Broglie wavelengths of a few **picometres**, about 100,000 times shorter than visible light. Their resolution is limited by lens aberrations and the sample, not by diffraction.
11. 22 nm. Part of the absorbed energy is lost as heat during vibrational relaxation before emission. A lower-energy photon means a longer wavelength ($E = hc/\lambda$).
12. $\Phi = 0.6$. Brightness ∝ ε·Φ: dye 1 gives $1\cdot0.6 = 0.6$; dye 2 gives $2\cdot0.3 = 0.6$. Equally bright.
13. $r = R_0$: $E = 0.5$. $r = 2R_0$: $E = \frac{1}{1 + 64}\approx0.015$. FRET efficiency changes dramatically between ~3 and 8 nm, so it reports **molecular proximity and conformational changes** (biosensors, protein interactions) at a scale below the diffraction limit.

## Level 4
14. $\left(\frac{920}{460}\right)^4 = 16$, so 920 nm light is scattered about 16 times less (in tissue the dependence is weaker, but the trend holds). Longer wavelengths also are absorbed less by haemoglobin. More ballistic light reaches deep layers.
15. **Photobleaching** (fluorophores destroyed, so the baseline drops) and **phototoxicity** (ROS and heat damage cells). Countermeasures: lower laser power or exposure (shorter, less frequent imaging), more sensitive detectors or higher NA (collect more photons), red-shifted or more photostable indicators, two-photon instead of one-photon excitation, and correcting baseline drift in the analysis.
16. $\sin\theta_c = \frac{1.45}{1.46}\approx0.993\Rightarrow\theta_c\approx83°$. $\text{NA} = \sqrt{1.46^2 - 1.45^2} = \sqrt{2.1316 - 2.1025} = \sqrt{0.0291}\approx0.17$. Only rays within a narrow cone (≈ ±10° in air) are guided, and that cone also determines how light spreads into the tissue.
