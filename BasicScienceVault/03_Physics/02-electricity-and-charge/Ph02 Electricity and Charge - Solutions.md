# Ph02 Electricity and Charge – Solutions

> Worksheet: [[Ph02 Electricity and Charge - Worksheet]] · Theory: [[Ph02 Electricity and Charge]] · Wrong? → [[Mistakes - Physics]]

## Level 1
1. $2e = 3.2\times10^{-19}\,\text{C}$; per mole $2F\approx1.93\times10^5\,\text{C}$.
2. $Q = I\cdot t = 10\,\text{C}$
3. Current is how much charge flows **through** a conductor per second (A = C/s). Voltage is the energy difference per charge **between** two points, the "push" that drives current (V = J/C). Voltage can exist without current; current needs voltage and a conducting path.
4. Voltages: −70 mV, 20 mV EPSP. Currents: 50 pA, 0.5 nA.

## Level 2
5. $F\propto1/r^2$, so $(1/3)^2 = 1/9$: the force drops to one-ninth.
6. $\varepsilon_r$ falls by a factor of 40, so the force increases 40-fold.
7. $F = 9\times10^9\cdot\frac{(1.6\times10^{-19})^2}{(10^{-9})^2} = 9\times10^9\cdot2.56\times10^{-20}\approx2.3\times10^{-10}\,\text{N}$ = 230 pN, **repulsive**. (In water it would be about 3 pN.)
8. It doubles, because one charge is $2e$ instead of $e$.

## Level 3
9. $\frac{10^{-12}}{1.6\times10^{-19}}\approx6.25\times10^6$ ions/s; for 1 nA, $6.25\times10^9$ ions/s.
10. $Q = 3\times10^{-12}\cdot2\times10^{-3} = 6\times10^{-15}\,\text{C}$, and $\frac{6\times10^{-15}}{1.6\times10^{-19}}\approx37\,500$ ions.
11. a) inward (negative) b) outward (positive) c) outward (positive, because negative charge enters) d) inward (negative). Depolarising: a) and d).
12. At the ion's equilibrium potential, the electrical force exactly balances the concentration gradient. The voltage is there, but the net driving force for that ion is zero ([[Ph03 Electric Fields and Potential]]).

## Level 4
13. The binding energy is $\propto\frac{1}{\varepsilon_r}$. In water ($\varepsilon_r = 80$) it falls to about the thermal energy, so ions separate, and water molecules additionally stabilise them by hydration. In lipid ($\varepsilon_r\approx2$) the attraction is 40 times stronger than in water and there is no hydration, so the ions stay paired, or rather never enter the lipid in the first place.
14. In the wire: **electrons**. In the solution: **ions** (Na⁺, K⁺, Cl⁻). At the surface, a redox reaction converts between them: $\text{AgCl} + e^-\rightleftharpoons\text{Ag} + \text{Cl}^-$. Electrons from the wire turn AgCl into Ag and release Cl⁻ into the solution, or the reverse.
15. a) $U = 9\times10^9\cdot\frac{2.56\times10^{-38}}{5\times10^{-10}}\approx4.6\times10^{-19}\,\text{J}\approx107\,kT$. b) $4.6\times10^{-19}/80\approx5.8\times10^{-21}\,\text{J}\approx1.3\,kT$. In vacuum the ion pair is extremely stable; in water, thermal collisions easily separate the ions, so the salt dissociates.
